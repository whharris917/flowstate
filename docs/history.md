# flowstate

A first-person factory-building simulation game set aboard a massive starship. The player builds a pharmaceutical plant from raw materials and bare bulkheads, one sensor, relay, and wire at a time, advised by an AI that sees only what the player sees and controls nothing.

**Read `docs/GDD.md` before doing any design or implementation work.** It is the single source of truth for vision, story, world, systems, and progression. It deliberately prescribes no build order (director, 2026-09-01): sequencing is a working decision recorded in this file, never a design commitment.

## Roles
- The human is the **creative director**. They set vision, review builds, and report what they saw. They do not write code and should not be asked to.
- Claude is the **entire development team**: architecture, code, scenes, tools, tests, docs. Make decisions, explain them briefly, and ask only when a choice is genuinely creative rather than technical.
- The director cannot see Claude's screen. Claude CAN see rendered output when needed: run a probe scene windowed (a real window briefly appears) that screenshots the viewport to `user://*.png` and read the PNG (`%APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\flowstate\`) — see `game/world/hud_probe.gd`. Use this to verify visual/UI work instead of guessing. Claude must also run Godot headlessly (see Environment) before every delivery: `--import` to catch parse errors, then a `--quit-after 600` smoke run to catch runtime errors and read the kernel self-check. Deliver things the director can open and press play, then ask for a build report ("what did you see, what felt wrong").

### Hydraulic solver gotchas (all eight cost real time to find)
- An undamped Newton step on a square law leaps over the answer and lands the same distance the other side, forever. Steps are halved until the imbalance improves.
- Regularising the *slope* near zero flow but not the *flow* leaves Newton chasing a target its own derivative disagrees with: it grinds against the iteration cap every tick and never lands. `_square_law_flow` and `_square_law_slope` must stay consistent. Getting this wrong cost 47 ms/tick and 60 iterations; right, it is 1.3 ms and 2.
- A region cut off from every fixed pressure (shut valve one side, blocked check valve the other) makes the matrix singular. Detect connectivity and take it out of the solve, or the solver abandons the whole plant because one corner is adrift.
- Such an island must then settle to ONE pressure, following only conducting branches, and **once, after the iteration**. Averaging across a stopped pump drags it toward the header it is isolated from and the leftover difference pushes material nothing supplied; doing it mid-solve moves pressures behind Newton's back and it never converges.
- **"Does it conduct?" and "what is its slope?" are different questions** (`is_conducting` vs `conductance`). A *shut check valve* reporting zero slope tells Newton the node is insensitive, so it steps as if only the pipe were there and sails clean over the pressure at which the valve cracks — then back, for ever. Report the open-side slope and it lands in two steps; the flow it passes while shut is still exactly zero, so nothing is invented. But connectivity must still treat it as a wall, or island detection breaks. Symptom: a pump feeding a vessel's **top** nozzle grinds at the iteration cap. It is geometry-dependent, so it reproduces at some vessel heights and not others — 2.4 m failed while 3.0 m was clean.
- **A `FixedFlow` is not a wall, it is the opposite of one.** Zero conductance, but material crosses it, so whatever it discharges into has a pressure to find. Read its flat slope as "not conducting" and the receiving node looks adrift, gets zeroed out of the Jacobian, and never moves: a dryer pushes cake at a hopper whose inlet check has not cracked yet, and the two wait for each other for ever.
- **An internal node with a single branch can carry no flow** — conservation says so. A component that gives its feed pump a private drum node and nothing else has built a dead end, and the machine can never start. Either join it to a nozzle node (the boiler drum *is* the steam nozzle) or make it a fixed boundary (the centrifuge bowl, the dryer drum, the still sump — they must be, because their discharges are imposed from last scan's split, and a machine that has never run has never drawn anything).
- **Record flows before settling islands, never after.** Settling averages an island's stale pressures; read a check valve at that average and it can open on paper and push material into a vessel from nowhere (1.9 L/s into a batch tank the moment its feed pump stopped). Flows are what the converged pressures say; settling is cosmetic and only zeroes an island's internal branches.
- **A trickle into a shut check valve cannot be Newton's problem.** A tenth of a millilitre a second arriving at a node whose only way out is a check that cracks 70 kPa away gives a step of a pascal or two, the damping loop finds no scale that helps, and the solve burns its full iteration cap every scan (619 of 24,000 in the showcase, and half the kernel's cost). When no scale of a step improves the residual, stop: re-linearising will not help either, and the imbalance is below anything the plant can see.
- **A pump on an empty vessel will drag its suction to the vacuum floor and pin there**, and a pinned node's imbalance can never be driven to zero, so the solve grinds for the rest of the run. Taper the curve to nothing over the last 20 kPa above hard vacuum — a taper, not a cut-off, because Newton cannot follow a cliff. And the taper's own derivative belongs in the Jacobian alongside the curve's: scaling only the conductance leaves it under-stepping at ~8 iterations instead of 2. The same trap as regularising a slope without its flow, wearing a different hat.
- **Convergence is judged per node, relative to what the node passes** (2026-09-20): the absolute tolerance of 1e-4 L/s was a tenth of a millilitre, and a drip line moving a tenth of a millilitre a second reported an open end at three times its own rotameter, both "converged". A node is done when its imbalance is under `min(1e-4, max(1e-8, 1e-3 × the sum of |Q| on it))`: never looser than before, tighter where the flows are small. Both kernels.
- **A step sized by the open side's slope can stop short of a plateau's edge** (2026-09-22): a dry nozzle or a shut check reports the open side's slope so Newton knows a wall is near, but the step that slope sizes (2Q·√(k·dp)) reaches the crack point only when the flow to push is large against the gap. The old fixed inlet stub (k 800) happened to clear it in the fill test; the Cv-sized nozzle (k 250) fell 300 Pa short, the norm could not improve at any halving because on the plateau every litre pushed toward the node lands in its residual, and the solve stopped with 1.6 L/s unbalanced. When no halving helps, both solvers now try **lengthening** the step (`LENGTHENINGS`: 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, one residual evaluation each) before giving up; the ladder climbs by 1.5 and 2 in turn because the window that improves the norm opens at the crack and closes where the open side overshoots (that case wanted 1.1 to 1.9) and doubling alone stepped over it.
- **A failed step lands at its shortest attempt, never back where it started** (2026-09-22, found the same afternoon): restoring the saved pressures exactly when no scale helps looked cleaner and broke the drip demo for good, because the regulator had shut a hair above its setpoint and reports zero slope shut, so from that exact point Newton had no way to reopen it; the 1/128 step the old code left behind was the nudge the next scan escaped by. A plateau whose slope reads zero (a shut regulator, a shut control valve) is escaped only by that nudge; the ladder above is for plateaus that report the open side's slope.
- **A branch whose flow depends on its two end pressures differently reports a slope per end** (`two_sided`, `gb` in GD, `conductance_b_at` in Python; the Jacobian takes both). The pressure regulator is one: its opening follows the downstream pressure alone, so its b-side slope is hundreds of times its a-side slope. Reporting their sum symmetrically told Newton the *inlet* was that stiff, the step failed every halving and the solve broke after one iteration with a tenth of a litre a second unbalanced; a scan-behind opening instead (a component adjusting a valve from last scan's pressure) swung shut and open for ever against a stiff downstream. Solved with the network and with the right slopes it lands in two iterations. The pump's prime term still goes on both sides; it is small.

- **A solve that stops short makes or loses material** (2026-09-22). Where Newton gives up, the recorded flows do not balance; at a vessel that is harmless (the vessel is credited with what its pipe carried), but at a free node inside a pass-through machine it is material from nowhere: E-301's shell lost 2.9 L in the showcase's first seconds while the worst node was T-403's, on the other side of the plant. So every solve now says whether it landed (`SimNetwork.converged` / Python `Network.converged`), the simulation counts the misses from t = 0 (`Simulation.unconverged_scans`, worst, where, when), every headless smoke and the showcase probe print `hydraulic solves that did not converge since t=0`, and the annunciator raises HYDRAULICS NOT CONVERGED within a second of one. Keep that line at "none" (the showcase reads 1, the cold-start scan, see below).
- **A plateau is crossed by stepping to its crack, not by Newton's slope** (2026-09-22). A node stranded below a closed one-way wall (a dry nozzle, a shut check, a one-way drain) with flow pushing at it sits on a flat stretch where nothing flows; Newton's local slope climbed a 10 kPa gap a few hundred pascals a scan (XV-401 onto T-402's dry roof, 20 scans) or kept improving a little and ran out the cap (XV-404 onto T-403, 56 kPa, 42 scans at up to 19.9 L/s). Each branch now answers `crack_target(node, pa, pb, push)`, where the node must stand to pass the flow pushing at it, and `_plateau_step` holds those nodes there and lets the linear solve move the rest; it competes with Newton's step every iteration and wins only on a smaller imbalance. The target inverts the branch's own law (`drop_for`, linear part included): a fixed pascal of margin through a 60 L/s open end is a thousand drips and the step is always refused. The mirror case, a node pulled against a wall that can never supply it (a pump on a vessel gone dry, a suction on a sewer), targets the hard-vacuum floor, where whatever pulls has tapered to nothing.
- **An imposed flow must starve** (2026-09-22). `FixedFlow` drew its rate regardless of suction, so the vial filler "filled" from a silo whose outlet stood above its liquid, every millilitre an imbalance no solve could close. It now tapers to nothing over the pump's 20 kPa above hard vacuum, its slope on the a side only (`two_sided`, `gb` 0). A machine drawing from its own fixed bowl or drum never nears vacuum and is unchanged.
- **A cut-off section keeps its equations** (2026-09-22). Frozen (a bare -1 row) and left out of the convergence test, as islands were, a false island stayed false: the drip line stranded between a regulator shut above its set point and a one-way open end shut below the air had liquid still pushing through it, nothing moved it, and the solve called it converged. Now an unreachable node keeps its row with a slight tie to where it stands (`ISLAND_TIE`, a millionth of its own slope, only to fix the island's common level), every node counts for convergence, and a false island moves until a wall reopens; a real dead leg comes to one pressure. Only a node with no slope at all is still dropped. This is what made the **open end one-way** (a raised end had drawn 35 L/s from the air in the hydraulics self-check): below the air at its height it passes nothing, like the drain.
- **A step must earn its length (Armijo)** (2026-09-22). A Newton step on a square law lands near the mirror image of where it started; a bare "less than" accepted it for a hair of improvement, and a stopped pump's suction flipped between the two for twenty iterations (the fill loop's balance drifted 0.37 L). `_improves(after, before, scale)` requires a cut of a ten-thousandth per unit of step, so the mirror is refused and the first halving lands.
- **A plateau step may be forced once a solve** (2026-09-22). Judged where it lands, a step to a wall's crack can look worse, since the node rose and what feeds it pushes harder for a moment; when Newton's own step has also failed, the crossing is taken anyway, once, and the next iteration settles from the open side (a tank's drain line started 38 kPa under the sewer and sat four scans). Forced, only a real crack: the vacuum-floor target suits a pump pulling, and a gravity line pulled against a dry nozzle simply stops.

- **A plant is many independent problems: solve them block by block** (2026-09-22). The free nodes fall into blocks joined by branches with both ends free, divided by the vessels and headers that fix pressures between them (`_blocks`; GD computes them with the ordering, keyed on the free set itself now, not its size — a node turning fixed as another turns free, a boiler's drum as it fires, left the old ordering wrong). One global Newton system is solved, but each block gets its own step length, judged on its own imbalance, and its own halvings, lengthenings and plateau step (`_block_step`); a block stops after **two** failed steps in a row (one failure is often a wall or a regulator that has just changed state, and the next linearisation is what it needs; the drip line's regulator cracked open on a failed step and a block stopped at the first failure ended Maine's cold solve 82 mL/s out). Found with the replay tool (below): one step length for the whole plant let the boiler's steam line swing across the drum pressure while Unit 400 improved enough to carry it, and when Unit 400 found no step that helped, the solve stopped with both unsettled.
- **A closed wall's slope counts only while flow is not pulled away from it** (2026-09-22). The open-side slope of a shut check, a dry nozzle or a one-way valve is right while flow pushes toward its crack (the check-valve lesson), and wrong when a node is pulled away: a line at rest between two dry nozzles sat with each end pinned at its own nozzle's crack and a valve between them passing a litre a second that could go nowhere. `_jacobian(…, residual)` / `_assemble(…, walls)` drop a closed wall's slope (closed by more than the 1 Pa linear stretch, where the law is smooth on purpose) from a node's row while that node's imbalance drives it away from the crack (`_pulled_away`); a row that lost a slope may lose its only anchor, so `_drop_dead` ties it (`ISLAND_TIE`) as it ties an island. Reporting no slope at all for closed walls was tried and was worse; so was tying every row (nanolitre leaks through shut valves).
- **A plateau has two landings** (2026-09-22): the crack with the arriving flow passing (a sustained feed) and the crack at rest (flow that is only the network settling — a cold boiler's drum and the sewer both at zero, whose trickle died as the line came up). `crack_target(…, passing)` gives both; `_plateau_step` tries both and keeps the better.
- **A converged solve takes one more step** (`_polish`, 2026-09-22): converged means each node within up to 0.1 mL/s, and inside a pass-through machine what is left is material made or lost; near the answer Newton is quadratic, so one full step, kept only if it improves, takes it to rounding. Only solves that iterated pay for it.
- **A rebuild keeps the answer it had** (2026-09-22). Every topology change (a placement, a removal, a line laid) builds a new network, and until now every one cold-started the whole plant from the seed; the Simulation now carries each existing nozzle's pressure into the new network by port path (`net.warm`), so only new nodes are seeded, and a save carries them too (`Simulation.port_pressures`/`load_pressures`, the plant snapshot's `pressures`), so a load starts where the plant was. A cold solve gets three times the iteration budget (`COLD_ITERATIONS` 60), once: a seeded start may legitimately need more than a warm one (the Maine site's drip line behind a shut regulator landed on its twentieth iteration). The seed is still the plant-wide mean of fixed pressures: a per-block mean and a unit-conductance linear solve were each tried and each found a case the mean does not (the linear seed started a node below a one-way drain's crack behind a tight orifice and the solve swung across it scan after scan). **Known and not yet fixed:** `tests/data/showcase_xv403_dead_leg.json` (an xfail in `test_replayed_networks.py`): the dead leg between Unit 400's barely-open XV-403 and its shut drain flips across the valve's square law every iteration, because a step is judged on the whole block and gains elsewhere in Unit 400 let the mirror image pass; reached only from a transient the per-block seed produced, but it is a real weakness waiting for the next layout that finds it.
- **Study a solve offline** (2026-09-22): `FLOWSTATE_NET_DUMP=<t>` on a smoke run makes the GD kernel write `user://net_dump_<t>.json` (`SimNetwork.to_dict()`, every node and branch as it stands before the solve at time t; every network in the run that passes t writes, the last one wins), and `tools/replay_network.py <dump>` rebuilds it in the Python kernel and solves it with a trace (`Network.trace`). The Python and GD solvers followed the same iterations to the digit on every dump tried. `tests/data/*.json` keeps the networks that once failed; `tests/test_replayed_networks.py` requires them to land.

## Architecture rules
- **The sim is data plus a tick.** Simulation logic lives in plain classes with declared inputs/outputs and `tick(dt)`. Nodes only render and take input. No game logic in `_process`.
- Two layers: **process** (material streams) and **signal** (24 V discrete, 4–20 mA analog, later fieldbus). Components bridge them.
- **Rich streams between units, deliberately simple equations inside them** (director's call, 2026-08-31). Material moves as a `Stream`: rate, temperature, composition (species fractions) and a suspended-solids fraction, conserved when streams merge, split and blend into a vessel. But the *inside* of a unit is a handful of algebraic and first-order relations — fractional conversion, a lumped energy balance, a split ratio. No discretized transport, no VLE, no film coefficients, ever. The two decisions reinforce each other: a unit you can describe in five equations is a unit you can put on a page in-game. Internals may be animated freely, but always off real state.
- **Pressure drives flow. Nothing asserts a flow rate** (director's call, 2026-08-31). Every material port is a node in a hydraulic network (`sim/hydraulics.py`); every pipe run is a branch with a resistance. Headers and vessels set boundary pressures, pumps add head on a real curve, valves resist per the valve equation, and what flows is whatever satisfies all of it at once. Consequences worth knowing: there is exactly one material port kind (`PROCESS_MATERIAL`), because with pressure solving direction there is nothing left for a second kind to protect against; a tee is a node where branches meet, and since 2026-09-12 that node is a **placeable fitting** (`tee_split` / `tee_mix`, `SimTee` / Python `Tee`, `SplitTee`, `MixTee`): four separated nozzles that `shared_node_ports()` maps to one hydraulic node, an unused one capped; and `PROCESS_LEVEL` / `PROCESS_PRESSURE` survive only as instrument taps.
- **One line per nozzle, one cable per terminal** (director, 2026-09-12: he saw lines lying inside each other at every shared inlet and outlet). `Plant.connect_equipment` refuses a visible wire onto a port that already carries one — "split it with a tee, or use another way" — for material and electrical alike; a tap reading a line does not count as a line on it, and hidden wires (mounts, cabinet internals, multicore circuits) are the plant's own bookkeeping. The device menu greys a wired outlet. The mains feeder has numbered ways (`ways` param, default 8; the showcase's has 24, `Plant.free_way` hands out the next free one); the seven junctions in the showcase are tee fittings now (`tee_solv`, `tee_308`, `tee_301a`, `tee_302d`, `tee_403a`, `tee_403m`, `tee_401m`). `Plant.fanout_report()` lists every port with more than one wire; the home-loop smoke prints it and the only entries left are instrument taps.
- **No draw wires.** The old facade had every consumer announce what it took so the supplier could decrement inventory. That was double-entry bookkeeping wearing the costume of a pipe, and it surfaced as a `draw` port on a supply header — which physically has no inlet at all. If you find yourself adding a port so the arithmetic works, that is the smell.
- Node pressures are **piezometric** (P + rho*g*z), so elevation costs head without the solver ever learning about the shape of a run. What the kernel knows of height is each record's own `elevation_m`: every boundary (vessel, header, drain, cap) and, since 2026-09-21, every inline pump and valve (the director: "a pump placed at the top of a rise or the bottom of a drop" must differ). An inline device reads its **static** pressures as the node less rho*g*z: the pump curve carries that datum (`datum_pa`) and judges prime and cavitation on the static suction, so a pump 9 m above an atmospheric tank makes two thirds of its curve and one 12 m above it nothing; the regulator's setting is static at its height; a valve's gauges are static and its drop unchanged. **Every gauge shows static pressure** (director, 2026-09-22: static "since it's *real*", piezometric being "a calculation convenience" and not a force over an area anyone can measure): a pump's, a valve's and the regulator's gauges convert at their own height; the pressure gauge (`press_kpa`) reads PROCESS_PRESSURE taps, and every one of those — the steam drum, the vacuum lock, the still's `p_top`, the cleanroom cascade — publishes a device's own gas pressure, static by nature, never a hydraulic node; a DP gauge's difference is the same in either frame. A new instrument that reads a liquid node must subtract ρg·z at its own elevation before it displays or transmits. **A tank's nozzles stand where they were welded** (director, 2026-09-22: "the tank's nozzle position should certainly be in the kernel"): each nozzle is a boundary node at the pressure it feels where it stands, `P_headspace + ρg(z + max(depth, h))`, and a `NozzleResistance` / `SimNozzleResistance` branch (vessel side a, line side b) whose outflow is scaled by its submergence (`clamp((depth − h)/0.03, 0, 1)`, the old uncover ramp) and whose inflow is always free — so under the liquid a nozzle carries head either way at any height (piezometric: two submerged nozzles read one pressure), above it it vents at headspace and passes nothing out, and a vessel keeps a heel below its outlet. Dry, it is a check seen from the vessel's side and reports the open side's slope (the check-valve lesson). `Tank.set_nozzle(port, height_m, dn)` in Python (defaults: outlet 0, inlet `AT_ROOF`, which follows a resize), `SimTank.set_nozzle_height` / `set_nozzle_dn` in GD, and **the one place the height is set in the game is `TankView._place_nozzle`**, so a weld, a resize and a load all land it (the build-api exercise welds one at half height, resizes, and reads it back; the hydraulics self-check drains a tank to the 0.3 m heel its outlet stands at). The reactor and crystallizer keep their fixed roof and floor nodes. **A nozzle takes the size of the line on it** (director, the same day: "nozzles should be auto-size-matched"): `Plant._sync_nozzle_bores`, run by `_sync_bores` for every record that is not an inline fitting, reads each material port's line (`_line_dn_on`, DN50 with none, `_pending_dn` before a first lay) and rebuilds that port's fitting at the line's bore — a tank through `TankView.set_nozzle_bore` (the nozzle keeps its own merged mesh), anything else by freeing its fittings and their merged meshes (`_free_markers`) and attaching them again with a per-port `bores` table (`attach_port_markers`); every fitting carries a `bore` meta that `_end_bore(name, port)` reads, so a line meets a nozzle of its own size flush and the reducer appears only where sizes really differ (a DN25 line into a tank whose other nozzle carries DN50 stays a DN25 nozzle). The kernel follows: a tank nozzle's Cv is `nozzle_cv_lps × (DN/50)²`, the bore's area, so the DN50 CONFIGURE figure is the reference and a DN6 nozzle passes 1.4 % of it; header, drain and pump nozzles change size only in the view.

## Conventions
- GDScript: static typing everywhere (`var level: float = 0.0`), snake_case, one class per file, `class_name` on sim classes.
- Python: 3.11, type hints, pytest, no heavy dependencies.
- **Writing pytest against the pressure kernel.** A test can no longer name a flow rate — flow is solved. Two shapes, and `tests/conftest.py` has the helpers for both. To test a *rate*, build a network and check the balance against the meters (`Source.total_l`, `Drain.total_l`) rather than a number you chose: `wire_power`, `wire_supply` (header + one pipe, no draw wire), `open_drain` (a discharge must go somewhere or the machine is dead-headed). To test a unit's *equations* on the bench, `feed(port, stream)` stands material at a nozzle the way the hydraulic pass leaves it — a signed `flow_lps` plus the composition at the node — and read the discharge back through `supplied_stream(name)`, which is the value the network would publish. Setting only `port.value` does nothing. Commands must be wired, never poked: `Duty` for analog, `Contact` for discrete, because an input port resets every scan. The full suite takes ~4 minutes; `test_hydraulics` and `test_flow_network` are the fast ones and the model for how the rest should read.
- Commit small, descriptive, and often. Never commit `.venv/` or `game/.godot/`.
- When delivering a build, end with: what changed, how to test it in Godot, and what to look for.
- **New equipment type checklist** (the standard rhythm, in order): (1) Python class in `sim/` + pytest; (2) an `EquipmentSpec` on that class — summary, a meaning for **every** port, equations, params, and an honest assumptions list; (3) GD mirror in `game/sim/` with `state_dict`/`apply_state` that round-trips its `contents` stream, and `build_hydraulics`/`update_hydraulics`/`supplied_stream`/`tap_ports` mirroring the Python's (a vessel is a fixed roof/floor pair, a machine with its own feed pump gets a fixed bowl or drum node, a discharge it imposes is a `SimFixedFlow`); (4) view in `game/components/` with describe()/use(), sim-edge audio, and any plumes; (5) `plant_factory.gd` — catalog entry, FOOTPRINTS, Y_OFFSETS, a CONFIG row per constructor param, PORT_ANCHORS (`{"pos","dir"}` per port; unanchored ports get an ugly default marker), make_record arm, make_view arm (a material nozzle needs nothing else: it is a node, and one pipe is one wire); (6) `plant.gd` — place() setup arm and `_params_for` if it has constructor params (else save/load loses them); give the record an `elevation_m` property if its pressures depend on its height — the plant re-derives it from where the record stands at placement, on a move and on a load for *every* record that has one (`Plant._apply_elevation`; director, 2026-09-21, after an open cap kept the vent height it was placed at because only five types were on the old list), and `NOZZLE_ELEVATION_TYPES` names the inline types whose nozzles stand a fixed height above their base; a boundary node's pressure must be refreshed in `update_hydraulics`, never only at build; (7) `asset_preview.gd` setup arm; (8) draft its library page by hand in `game/sim/sim_library_data.gd` for the director's editorial review (see the library rule above); (9) commission one in `showcase.gd` (wired, powered, running), and add its type to a rung's `unlocks` in `data/milestones.gd` or the campaign can never build it; (10) full gauntlet: pytest, `--import`, both-world smoke runs, showcase probe + library probe, and read the screenshots.
- **The build palette** (director, 2026-09-19: "icons along the left for the build menu, and a numberkey shortcut menu on the bottom (player-defined) ... not to have to rely so much on tabbing through build menus"): `ui/build_palette.gd` (`BuildPalette`) is a rail of the seven page icons down the left (each page's first entry, rendered by `AssetIcons`, refreshed as thumbnails land) and a **hotbar** of nine slots along the bottom; `BuildMenu` cards are clickable and report the hovered card. **Tab** opens the palette (mouse free, the player stands still) and closes it; **B** opens it from play or leaves build mode; clicking a page icon changes page, clicking a card picks that type and goes back to the crosshair, a click outside the panels goes back too, a right click leaves build mode. **The number keys are the hotbar**: `BuildController.hotbar` holds nine types (a fresh install starts with tank, pump, control valve, header, drain, block valve, flow gauge, cabinet, feeder), a number picks its slot from anywhere in play (`_pick_slot`, `_locate` finding the page; a locked type says so), and with the palette open a number over a hovered card puts that type in the slot (`_assign_slot`), saved under `hotbar` in `user://settings.json`. ### As of 2026-09-01 — the kernels

**Both kernels run on pressure.** The GDScript kernel under `game/sim/` was ported from `sim/` on 2026-09-01, the same shape as the Python: `SimNetwork` (nodal Newton) and `SimBranch` with its five kinds (`SimResistance`, `SimCheckResistance`, `SimControlResistance`, `SimPumpCurve`, `SimFixedFlow`), one material port kind, no draw wires, vessel nozzles with elevation, instrument taps. `Simulation.tick()` solves the network between signal propagation and the component ticks, exactly as `sim/core.py` does. One player pipe is one kernel wire and the facade pairing is gone. Every record with an `elevation_m` property takes it from where it stands — at placement, on a move and on a load — through `Plant._apply_elevation` (a vessel, header or drain from its base, an inline pump or valve from its nozzle height above its base per `NOZZLE_ELEVATION_TYPES`, a cap from its line), so a tank on a deck genuinely stands above one at grade; a saved `elevation_m` in params wins on a load. Saves are v5; v3/v4 saves still load, minus any wire to a port that no longer exists.

The headless gauntlet is six checks in `plant.gd` (**headless only** since 2026-09-12: they simulate ten minutes of plant and cost about eight seconds, which was the wait after the title screen; every world prints a `[flowstate] startup … ms` line with its phases, so a slow start is measured, not guessed): kernel loop (the fill loop cycles and closes its balance to the millilitre), **hydraulics** (a tee splits by resistance and conserves; a pump makes rated flow, dead-heads, and two in parallel do not double; a raised tank drains into a low one with no pump; gravity will not run uphill; header -> tank -> drain closes; a valve half open passes more than half; two headers blend), control, streams, build-api, support. The showcase probe soaks Unit 300 for twenty simulated minutes and reports **100.0 % closed** with the boiler's makeup counted as material entering the plant, plus the network size, band, iterations and solve time.

Sizing decisions, confirmed with the director 2026-09-01, both kept as they were:
- **Valve sizing basis**: `cv_lps` = flow at full open across a 1 bar drop. Deliberately *not* US Cv (gpm at 1 psi) or metric Kv — it reads in the game's own units, so nothing converts.
- **Pump curve**: the generic `H = H0 * (1 - (Q/Qmax)^2)`, runout capped at 1.35x rated. No efficiency, no BEP. Cavitation is a suction-pressure taper rather than NPSH, because the species table carries no vapour pressure.

**How the GD solver differs from the Python** (same algorithm, same answers; these are the port's own lessons, all about GDScript cost):
- The Newton system is solved within a band after a reverse Cuthill-McKee ordering of the free nodes, computed once per topology, with no pivoting — the Jacobian is symmetric and diagonally dominant, and a dead node is a bare -1 on the diagonal. The showcase's band is 1: free nodes only ever chain through fixed vessel nodes. The dense pivoting elimination the Python uses cost 15 ms a scan here.
- `SimBranch.evaluate(pa, pb)` answers flow, slope and connectivity in one call into the fields `q`, `g`, `conducting`; the solver reads the fields, and a damped step's evaluation is reused at the top of the next iteration. Three calls per branch per iteration was half the solve.
- `_resolve_compositions` accumulates arrivals per node in flat arrays instead of building a stream per branch; a vessel returns its `contents` from `supplied_stream` and the caller sets the rate; a nozzle with one run on it reuses the node stream rather than copying it.
- Cost on the full sandbox (93 nodes, 78 branches): about 11 ms a scan through the startup transient (~5 iterations) and about 5 ms at steady state (2 iterations), of which the Newton solve is roughly half and the composition pass a third; the rest is component ticks and ~700 historian tags. The budget is 50 ms at 20 Hz. The `[flowstate] kernel cost` line in every smoke run is the number to watch. Next levers, if it ever matters: skip re-evaluating branches whose end pressures did not move, and stop halving the damped step once the residual is within a few tolerances.

**Known gap, worth a decision:** mass does not close across the steam generator. Its drum is a fixed-pressure boundary (like a supply header), so it hands out whatever steam is drawn and makes up the difference from nowhere — its pressure sags with *feedwater*, not with demand. In the showcase this is now visible in the numbers: a 700 kPa drum pushes about 1.2 kg/s through the exchanger shell from 0.43 kg/s of feedwater, and the probe prints the makeup rather than hiding it. Stated plainly on the boiler's page. Closing it properly means giving the drum a real inventory, which replaces the `P_target = P_full * feed/rated` heuristic; cheap to do, but it changes the boiler's dynamics, so it is the director's call. Everywhere else in the plant the balance closes to ~0.005 %.

**The Python reference** runs the recycle ring at ~5 ms/tick (51 nodes), unoptimised: `Stream` construction and `_resolve_compositions` dominate. It is the reference, not what the game runs, and it is fine as it is.
