# flowstate

A first-person factory-building simulation game set aboard a massive starship. The player builds a pharmaceutical plant from raw materials and bare bulkheads, one sensor, relay, and wire at a time, advised by an AI that sees only what the player sees and controls nothing.

**Read `docs/GDD.md` before doing any design or implementation work.** It is the single source of truth for vision, story, world, systems, and progression. It deliberately prescribes no build order (director, 2026-09-01): sequencing is a working decision recorded in this file, never a design commitment.

### Hydraulic solver gotchas (all eight cost real time to find)
- An undamped Newton step on a square law leaps over the answer and lands the same distance the other side, forever. Steps are halved until the imbalance improves.
- Regularising the *slope* near zero flow but not the *flow* leaves Newton chasing a target its own derivative disagrees with: it grinds against the iteration cap every tick and never lands. `_square_law_flow` and `_square_law_slope` must stay consistent. Getting this wrong cost 47 ms/tick and 60 iterations; right, it is 1.3 ms and 2.
- A region cut off from every fixed pressure (shut valve one side, blocked check valve the other) makes the matrix singular. Detect connectivity and take it out of the solve, or the solver abandons the whole plant because one corner is adrift.
- Such an island must then settle to ONE pressure, following only conducting branches, and **once, after the iteration**. Averaging across a stopped pump drags it toward the header it is isolated from and the leftover difference pushes material nothing supplied; doing it mid-solve moves pressures behind Newton's back and it never converges.
- **"Does it conduct?" and "what is its slope?" are different questions** (`is_conducting` vs `conductance`). A *shut check valve* reporting zero slope tells Newton the node is insensitive, so it steps as if only the pipe were there and sails clean over the pressure at which the valve cracks — then back, for ever. Report the open-side slope and it lands in two steps; the flow it passes while shut is still exactly zero, so nothing is invented. But connectivity must still treat it as a wall, or island detection breaks. Symptom: a pump feeding a vessel's **top** nozzle grinds at the iteration cap. It is geometry-dependent, so it reproduces at some vessel heights and not others — 2.4 m failed while 3.0 m was clean.
- **A `FixedFlow` is not a wall, it is the opposite of one.** Zero conductance, but material crosses it, so whatever it discharges into has a pressure to find. Read its flat slope as "not conducting" and the receiving node looks adrift, gets zeroed out of the Jacobian, and never moves: a dryer pushes cake at a hopper whose inlet check has not cracked yet, and the two wait for each other for ever.
- **An internal node with a single branch can carry no flow** — conservation says so. A component that gives its feed pump a private drum node and nothing else has built a dead end, and the machine can never start. Either join it to a nozzle node (the boiler drum *is* the steam nozzle) or make it a fixed boundary (the centrifuge bowl, the dryer drum, the still sump — they must be, because their discharges are imposed from last scan's split, and a machine that has never run has never drawn anything).
- **A pump on an empty vessel will drag its suction to the vacuum floor and pin there**, and a pinned node's imbalance can never be driven to zero, so the solve grinds for the rest of the run. Taper the curve to nothing over the last 20 kPa above hard vacuum — a taper, not a cut-off, because Newton cannot follow a cliff. And the taper's own derivative belongs in the Jacobian alongside the curve's: scaling only the conductance leaves it under-stepping at ~8 iterations instead of 2. The same trap as regularising a slope without its flow, wearing a different hat.

## Architecture rules
- **The sim is data plus a tick.** Simulation logic lives in plain classes with declared inputs/outputs and `tick(dt)`. Nodes only render and take input. No game logic in `_process`.
- Two layers: **process** (material streams) and **signal** (24 V discrete, 4–20 mA analog, later fieldbus). Components bridge them.
- **Rich streams between units, deliberately simple equations inside them** (director's call, 2026-08-31). Material moves as a `Stream`: rate, temperature, composition (species fractions) and a suspended-solids fraction, conserved when streams merge, split and blend into a vessel. But the *inside* of a unit is a handful of algebraic and first-order relations — fractional conversion, a lumped energy balance, a split ratio. No discretized transport, no VLE, no film coefficients, ever. The two decisions reinforce each other: a unit you can describe in five equations is a unit you can put on a page in-game. Internals may be animated freely, but always off real state.
- **Pressure drives flow. Nothing asserts a flow rate** (director's call, 2026-08-31). Every material port is a node in a hydraulic network (`sim/hydraulics.py`); every pipe run is a branch with a resistance. Headers and vessels set boundary pressures, pumps add head on a real curve, valves resist per the valve equation, and what flows is whatever satisfies all of it at once. Consequences worth knowing: there is exactly one material port kind (`PROCESS_MATERIAL`), because with pressure solving direction there is nothing left for a second kind to protect against; **a tee is not a component**, it is a node where branches meet; and `PROCESS_LEVEL` / `PROCESS_PRESSURE` survive only as instrument taps.
- **No draw wires.** The old facade had every consumer announce what it took so the supplier could decrement inventory. That was double-entry bookkeeping wearing the costume of a pipe, and it surfaced as a `draw` port on a supply header — which physically has no inlet at all. If you find yourself adding a port so the arithmetic works, that is the smell.
- Node pressures are **piezometric** (P + rho*g*z), so elevation costs head without the solver ever learning about elevation. Vessel nozzles are real: a top nozzle discharges above the liquid and cannot flow backwards, a bottom nozzle stops when the level falls past it.
- **The equipment library is hand-authored, under the director's editorial review** (director, 2026-09-01; this supersedes the generated pipeline). The in-game pages live in `game/sim/sim_library_data.gd` as prose the director edits and signs off. They are **not** derived from the Python `EquipmentSpec`s and are not required to match the Python source. `tools/generate_library.py` has been removed and must not come back in any form: no generated page text, no tooling that writes that file. When a new type is added, Claude drafts its page in that file and hands it to the director for review. Two things survive from the old pipeline: port NAMES, kinds and directions are still read off the live GD record by `sim_library.gd`, so a page cannot list I/O the machine does not have; and `world/library_probe.tscn` still reports pages missing prose, an equation, or a meaning for a real port, which is an editorial to-do list rather than a build failure. The Python `EquipmentSpec` stays as the Python kernel's own documentation and the only copy of its equations — `sim/library.py` and `tests/test_library.py` read it — but it is Python-side only.
- Fixed-rate deterministic tick. No frame-rate dependence.
- Every visual object in the world is backed by a sim record. No decorative equipment.
- **The historian is first-class.** Every sim variable is a tag, sampled every tick into a time-series record. All displays — trend pages, HMIs, gauges — read from the historian or live sim state. Displayed data is never faked, smoothed, or hand-tuned; it must be an accurate reflection of simulated process conditions, as if read from a plant historian (PI / DeltaV Process History View).
- **Every feature ships visual, as a GUI.** A delivery is not done until the director can *see* the process behave: during the Python phase, a live browser GUI (stdlib http server + HTML/JS) with operator graphics and trends of the real historized data; in-game HMI trends later. Include how to launch it.
- Save/load serializes the sim graph. Keep it working from the first playable.
- Placeholder art only (boxes, capsules, flat color) until systems are proven.
- Prototype new mechanics in `sim/` (Python) first when it is cheaper to validate there; port to GDScript once the design is settled.

## Conventions
- GDScript: static typing everywhere (`var level: float = 0.0`), snake_case, one class per file, `class_name` on sim classes.
- Python: 3.11, type hints, pytest, no heavy dependencies.
- **Writing pytest against the pressure kernel.** A test can no longer name a flow rate — flow is solved. Two shapes, and `tests/conftest.py` has the helpers for both. To test a *rate*, build a network and check the balance against the meters (`Source.total_l`, `Drain.total_l`) rather than a number you chose: `wire_power`, `wire_supply` (header + one pipe, no draw wire), `open_drain` (a discharge must go somewhere or the machine is dead-headed). To test a unit's *equations* on the bench, `feed(port, stream)` stands material at a nozzle the way the hydraulic pass leaves it — a signed `flow_lps` plus the composition at the node — and read the discharge back through `supplied_stream(name)`, which is the value the network would publish. Setting only `port.value` does nothing. Commands must be wired, never poked: `Duty` for analog, `Contact` for discrete, because an input port resets every scan. The full suite takes ~4 minutes; `test_hydraulics` and `test_flow_network` are the fast ones and the model for how the rest should read.
- Commit small, descriptive, and often. Never commit `.venv/` or `game/.godot/`.
- When delivering a build, end with: what changed, how to test it in Godot, and what to look for.
- **New equipment type checklist** (the standard rhythm, in order): (1) Python class in `sim/` + pytest; (2) an `EquipmentSpec` on that class — summary, a meaning for **every** port, equations, params, and an honest assumptions list; (3) GD mirror in `game/sim/` with `state_dict`/`apply_state` that round-trips its `contents` stream, and `build_hydraulics`/`update_hydraulics`/`supplied_stream`/`tap_ports` mirroring the Python's (a vessel is a fixed roof/floor pair, a machine with its own feed pump gets a fixed bowl or drum node, a discharge it imposes is a `SimFixedFlow`); (4) view in `game/components/` with describe()/use(), sim-edge audio, and any plumes; (5) `plant_factory.gd` — catalog entry, FOOTPRINTS, Y_OFFSETS, PORT_ANCHORS (`{"pos","dir"}` per port; unanchored ports get an ugly default marker), make_record arm, make_view arm (a material nozzle needs nothing else: it is a node, and one pipe is one wire); (6) `plant.gd` — place() setup arm and `_params_for` if it has constructor params (else save/load loses them); add its type to the list in place() that takes `elevation_m` from the placement height if it has nozzles that care; (7) `asset_preview.gd` setup arm; (8) draft its library page by hand in `game/sim/sim_library_data.gd` for the director's editorial review (see the library rule above); (9) commission one in `showcase.gd` (wired, powered, running); (10) full gauntlet: pytest, `--import`, both-world smoke runs, showcase probe + library probe, and read the screenshots.
- Build pages are capped at 9 entries (number-key selection). Adding a page means touching `_catalog()`, `_is_equipment_page()`, `page_names` and the `% N` wrap in `build_controller.gd` — all four, or the pages silently misalign.

## Where things stand (2026-09-01 — READ THIS FIRST)

**Both kernels run on pressure.** The GDScript kernel under `game/sim/` was ported from `sim/` on 2026-09-01, the same shape as the Python: `SimNetwork` (nodal Newton) and `SimBranch` with its five kinds (`SimResistance`, `SimCheckResistance`, `SimControlResistance`, `SimPumpCurve`, `SimFixedFlow`), one material port kind, no draw wires, vessel nozzles with elevation, instrument taps. `Simulation.tick()` solves the network between signal propagation and the component ticks, exactly as `sim/core.py` does. One player pipe is one kernel wire and the facade pairing is gone. A vessel, header or drain takes its `elevation_m` from the placement height (re-derived from the saved position on load), so a tank on a deck genuinely stands above one at grade. Saves are v5; v3/v4 saves still load, minus any wire to a port that no longer exists.

The headless gauntlet is six checks in `plant.gd`: kernel loop (the fill loop cycles and closes its balance to the millilitre), **hydraulics** (a tee splits by resistance and conserves; a pump makes rated flow, dead-heads, and two in parallel do not double; a raised tank drains into a low one with no pump; gravity will not run uphill; header -> tank -> drain closes; a valve half open passes more than half; two headers blend), control, streams, build-api, support. The showcase probe soaks Unit 300 for twenty simulated minutes and reports **100.0 % closed** with the boiler's makeup counted as material entering the plant, plus the network size, band, iterations and solve time.

Sizing decisions, confirmed with the director 2026-09-01, both kept as they were:
- **Valve sizing basis**: `cv_lps` = flow at full open across a 1 bar drop. Deliberately *not* US Cv (gpm at 1 psi) or metric Kv — it reads in the game's own units, so nothing converts.
- **Pump curve**: the generic `H = H0 * (1 - (Q/Qmax)^2)`, runout capped at 1.35x rated. No efficiency, no BEP. Cavitation is a suction-pressure taper rather than NPSH, because the species table carries no vapour pressure.

**How the GD solver differs from the Python** (same algorithm, same answers; these are the port's own lessons, all about GDScript cost):
- The Newton system is solved within a band after a reverse Cuthill-McKee ordering of the free nodes, computed once per topology, with no pivoting — the Jacobian is symmetric and diagonally dominant, and a dead node is a bare -1 on the diagonal. The showcase's band is 1: free nodes only ever chain through fixed vessel nodes. The dense pivoting elimination the Python uses cost 15 ms a scan here.
- `SimBranch.evaluate(pa, pb)` answers flow, slope and connectivity in one call into the fields `q`, `g`, `conducting`; the solver reads the fields, and a damped step's evaluation is reused at the top of the next iteration. Three calls per branch per iteration was half the solve.
- `_resolve_compositions` accumulates arrivals per node in flat arrays instead of building a stream per branch; a vessel returns its `contents` from `supplied_stream` and the caller sets the rate; a nozzle with one run on it reuses the node stream rather than copying it.
- Cost on the full sandbox (93 nodes, 78 branches): about 11 ms a scan through the startup transient (~5 iterations) and about 5 ms at steady state (2 iterations), of which the Newton solve is roughly half and the composition pass a third; the rest is component ticks and ~700 historian tags. The budget is 50 ms at 20 Hz. The `[flowstate] kernel cost` line in every smoke run is the number to watch. Next levers, if it ever matters: skip re-evaluating branches whose end pressures did not move, and stop halving the damped step once the residual is within a few tolerances.

**Known gap, worth a decision:** mass does not close across the steam generator. Its drum is a fixed-pressure boundary (like a supply header), so it hands out whatever steam is drawn and makes up the difference from nowhere — its pressure sags with *feedwater*, not with demand. In the showcase this is now visible in the numbers: a 700 kPa drum pushes about 1.2 kg/s through the exchanger shell from 0.43 kg/s of feedwater, and the probe prints the makeup rather than hiding it. Stated plainly on the boiler's page. Closing it properly means giving the drum a real inventory, which replaces the `P_target = P_full * feed/rated` heuristic; cheap to do, but it changes the boiler's dynamics, so it is the director's call. Everywhere else in the plant the balance closes to ~0.005 %.

**The Python reference** runs the recycle ring at ~5 ms/tick (51 nodes), unoptimised: `Stream` construction and `_resolve_compositions` dominate. It is the reference, not what the game runs, and it is fine as it is.
